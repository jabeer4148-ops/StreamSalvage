Phone Died While Recording? Recover iPhone or Android Video

By Abdul Jabeer Shaik · Updated September 26, 2026

Charge and restart your phone, then check whether the original video exists and plays. A missing clip, an incomplete download, and an unplayable recording need different fixes. Keep the original file and avoid deleting the recording app while you investigate.

Quick check: if the video plays on the phone but fails on your computer, try transferring the original again. If it is missing everywhere, look for a saved or backed-up copy first. Repair tools need an existing file with media data.

My iPhone died while recording a video: what should I check?

  1. With the phone charged, reopen Photos and the app you used to record. Check whether a clip was saved and whether it plays.
  2. If it is missing, check Recently Deleted, Hidden, and your library views. If you already used iCloud Photos, check whether the clip exists there or on another synced device.
  3. If you find an original, preserve it before editing or converting it. Try playback on the phone and on a computer.
  4. If only the computer copy fails, export or copy the original again and let the transfer complete.

Apple documents these places to look in its missing photos and videos guide. Checking an existing backup can recover a saved copy; it cannot restore video the phone never wrote. Charging overnight should not be treated as a guaranteed repair method.

Android: check camera and screen-recorder files

Look in your gallery, the recorder app's saved recordings, and its output folder using the phone's Files app. Folder names vary by app and device. Avoid clearing app data or reinstalling the recorder before you have copied any surviving files.

If you used Google Photos, check its library, archive, and trash. Google's deleted-video recovery instructions explain restoring items that are still in trash. A video that never finished saving may not have been backed up.

For a damaged saved screen recording, see the screen recording troubleshooting guide. If the original plays but a transferred copy fails, retry the transfer before attempting repair.

Try local repair for an existing corrupted MP4

  1. Copy the original to a computer and make a working duplicate. Keep the original phone copy where possible.
  2. Find a healthy clip made with the same phone and recording app. Match video codec, resolution, frame rate, and audio settings. If recording a new reference, first preserve all surviving material.
  3. Try a repair tool that supports the file and codec, supplying both the working copy and reference if requested.
  4. Check the output's duration, picture, sound, and sync at several points before relying on it.

A reference can help interpret surviving media. It cannot restore missing frames, and phones can produce different formats even with similar settings. Do not rename a MOV to MP4 and assume it has been converted. See reference-file matching and missing MP4 metadata.

When to stop trying container repair

If there is no saved file, the file is empty, or storage is failing, repeating MP4 repairs will not create the missing media. Preserve remaining copies and consider device-specific recovery advice. For future recordings, start with sufficient charge and storage, test a short clip, and back up important completed videos.

Frequently asked questions

My iPhone died while recording and the video is a black thumbnail. Is it gone?

A black thumbnail alone cannot tell you whether video data survives. Charge the phone, check the original in Photos or the recording app, and look for existing backups. Preserve any file you find. If a non-empty recording remains unreadable, repair may be worth trying on a copy.

How do I fix a corrupted screen recording on Android?

First find the saved recording in the recorder app or its output folder and preserve a copy. If it plays on the phone, retry the transfer or check the computer player. If a non-empty MP4 is damaged, try a compatible repair tool with a reference clip from the same recorder and settings.

Why does a phone video stop playing after transfer?

The transfer may be incomplete, or the destination player may not support the codec. Check whether the original still plays, compare the copied file, and transfer it again before treating it as corrupted.
